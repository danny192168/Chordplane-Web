Built for playing

Four things a paper sheet can’t do.

The tools stay folded away until you reach for them. What is left on screen is the song, sized for a music stand and readable under stage lights.

  1. 01
    Sheet

    Chords, exactly where they land

    ChordPro brackets, plain-text chord rows, or a sheet pasted from anywhere. The chord sits above the syllable you play it on, so the page reads like a lead sheet instead of a wall of brackets.

  2. 02
    Transpose

    Change key, not the sheet

    Nudge the whole song from −11 to +11 semitones. Every chord, every diagram and the capo hint move together, and the sheet stays as legible as it was in the original key.

  3. 03
    Diagrams

    See the shape before you play it

    Tap any chord for guitar or ukulele voicings, or open every chord in the song at once. Each diagram is drawn as you read, so it works offline and at any text size.

  4. 04
    Set lists

    A running order you can trust

    Group songs into a set list, drag them into the order you will play them, and open the next song with one tap. The list never needs a connection.

Bring your own

The sheets you already have still work.

Paste a lead sheet with bracketed chords, a plain-text chord row, a ChordPro export from another app, or a list copied out of a message. ChordPlane detects the format, keeps the sections, and lays it out as a sheet you can play from.

  • ChordPro ([G]) and plain chord rows
  • Section labels, repeats, capo and tempo
  • Nothing is uploaded to read it

Privacy

Nothing leaves the phone unless you share it.

No analytics, no adverts, no background sync. Your library is a database file on your device, and it stays there.

Read the full privacy note →
  • An account is optional — it exists so a share link can be traced back to you and deleted later.
  • A share link stores exactly the sheet you sent, not the rest of your library.
  • Google sign-in receives only your email address, name and profile picture.
